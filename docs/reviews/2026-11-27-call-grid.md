# The call grid: where our system has no meaning (2026-11-27)

Rick (2026-11-27): "yes I'd like to fill in the grid. This will get out of hand if we
try all competitive auctions though, but it would be good to have a general description
of where the holes are. All bidding systems have holes like that - not a problem in
itself - but cross-check the common ones against Brill, WBridge5 and GIB to see if it
looks like we are missing anything basic."

This is phase 1: the grid and the cross-check, rules untouched. The full tables are in
[2026-11-27-call-grid/tables.md](2026-11-27-call-grid/tables.md); the tool is
`probes/tools/call_grid.py` (walk, comp, weigh, cross, covers, report). The holes that
real auctions run into are in [2026-11-27-undefined-calls.md](2026-11-27-undefined-calls.md).

## How it was done

- **Our tree, uncontested.** From the opening seat and the second seat, the opponents
  passing throughout, every position our own rules can reach up to the fourth non-pass
  call: 4,408 positions on the `Brill` card (4,299 on 21GF-GIB). At each, the engine's own
  candidate list (`rbb bid rules --json`, before the hand is known) gives the calls with a
  meaning; every other legal bid up to 5NT is a hole. Pack `bottom-review` a3bffe8.
- **Competition, one level only.** Responder over each of our openings and their simple
  action; our first call over each of their openings, direct and pass-out seat; the
  advancer over a few common overcalls and doubles. 108 positions, a description, not a tree.
- **Weights.** Every position and call counted in real auctions: the Brill field (50 days,
  all tables), the BBA and GIB corpora, and WBridge5 at all four seats (1,199 deals already
  on disk; the VM was not started).
- **Cross-check.** 114 uncontested holes (the 60 that real auctions use most, plus every
  hole in rounds 1-2 up to 4♠ in first seat) and 234 competitive holes (through 3NT, at
  positions the Brill field reached ten times or more), against GIB's tree (the gibrest
  archive; a few positions fetched), Brill's `/explain` (Thorvald's service, one request a
  second, cached; about 290 new requests) and WBridge5's own auctions.

Reading the cross-check: GIB's tree answers almost every call (a natural description
when nothing more specific applies), and Brill infers a natural meaning for calls no rule
set names (`Inferred (...)`). So "has a meaning" is not the test. **Basic** = a named Brill
rule set reads the call *and* GIB has it: both systems carry it explicitly, so we are
probably missing something. **Convention-dependent** = only one of them names it.
**Nobody** = Brill only infers it and GIB only describes it: fine, or a natural meaning
nobody wrote down.

## In short

1. **Structurally we define about half of all legal bids up to 5NT** (16,482 of 32,404;
   two thirds through 3NT). That is the "every system has holes" part: most holes are
   jumps to the four and five level that nobody plays.
2. **What real auctions actually bid at our positions, we nearly always read**: 99% of
   responses, 98% of opener's rebids, 86% of responder's rebids in the Brill field (80-90%
   in the BBA and GIB corpora, 73% for WBridge5, whose system differs most).
3. **But the cross-check finds real basics missing**, in two places above all:
   - **responding to partner's preempts** (weak twos, three- and four-level openings):
     new suits, 3NT to play, 4M to play are mostly undefined;
   - **competition one level in**: the pass-out seat beyond the one level, anything over
     their strong 2♣, responder's calls after interference over our weak twos, our 2♣ and
     our 1NT, and responder's new suits after their takeout double.
   Uncontested: 86 of 114 checked holes are basic, 2 convention-dependent, 26 nobody's.
   Competitive: 149 of 234 basic, 23 convention-dependent, 62 nobody's.
4. The in-flight branches barely touch these: `gf-after-2nt` covers 4 (opener's game
   after Jacoby 2NT continuations and 1♥-3♠), `second-calls-defence` none (it is about the
   overcaller's and doubler's second calls, which this grid does not reach), and
   `second-calls-opener` was not pushed yet, so it could not be asked.

## Where the holes are, in general terms

- **Openings.** Only 5♥, 5♠ and 5NT have no meaning.
- **Responses.** After 1♥/1♠/1NT/2NT nearly everything to game is defined. After 1♣
  and 1♦ the holes are the double jumps (1♣-3♦/3♥/3♠, 1♦-3♥/3♠) and the jumps to four
  (4♣-4♠). After weak twos and three-level openings most new suits, 3NT and the jumps to
  game are holes: 2♥-3♣/3♦/3♠/3NT/4♣/4♦/4♠, 3♠-3NT/4♣/4♦/4♥ and the like.
- **Rebids, through 3NT.** By weight the holes are: new suits at the three level and jump
  new suits (opener's 5-5 jump reverse, responder's 3-level fourth suit and new suits
  after opener's 2NT or 2M rebid), the cheapest NT and simple preferences at responder's
  second turn, and rebids of a long suit after Stayman.
- **Rebids above 3NT.** Mostly four- and five-level jumps (to-play or splinter calls
  nobody plays the same way), and 4NT/5NT outside the keycard and quantitative auctions.
- **Competition, one level.** The pass-out seat stops at the one level: 2-level jump
  overcalls, the Michaels cue and everything at the three level are undefined. Over their
  strong 2♣ we have no call at all (0 of 10). Over interference of our weak twos and our
  2♣, responder has one or two calls defined. Over our 1NT and their 2♣ or 2♦, half the
  calls are missing (Lebensohl-type 2NT, 3M stopper and game calls). After their double of
  our one of a suit, responder's two-level new suits, Jordan 2NT, preemptive jumps and
  3NT are missing. After their overcall, responder's jumps to three in a new suit.
- **Passes.** At 1,114 positions no rule makes a pass that no force rules out: the engine
  falls back to "No rule applies" there. Most are after a game or slam try; not looked at
  further here.

## Proposed fill-in, by family (phase 2, for Rick's go)

Standard meanings first; where the card has a field the rule reads it; Brill-card
specifics are flagged.

1. **Responding to partner's preempts**: new suit forcing over a weak two (RONF) and over
   three-level openings, 3NT to play, 4M to play, 4m over 3m as preemptive. Standard, all
   cards. The largest uncontested family, and Brill robots preempt opposite Rusty often.
2. **The pass-out seat beyond the one level**: intermediate jump overcalls, the Michaels
   cue, natural three-level calls, 3NT natural; with the borrowed king
   (BIDDING-MODEL 3.8). Standard.
3. **Responder after their takeout double of our one of a suit**: two-level free new
   suits, Jordan/Truscott 2NT, 3m preemptive raise, 3NT. Weak jump shifts over a double are
   a card setting (`vs_to_double.jump_shift`, off on every stock card): Rick's call
   whether the Brill card plays them (Brill and GIB both read them as preemptive).
4. **Interference over our weak twos, our 2♣ and our 1NT**: over (X) new suits forcing,
   XX, 2NT still the ask, 3NT; over their overcall 2NT, new suits, 3NT, penalty X; over
   our 2♣ positives and a penalty X; over our 1NT and their two-level bid, Lebensohl is a
   convention (card field): flag for Rick.
5. **Defending their strong 2♣**: natural overcalls, preempts, a lead-directing double.
   Standard, cheap.
6. **Natural second calls**: opener's 5-5 jump reverse, Stayman continuations (3m natural
   GF, 3OM slam try, 4m splinters), the three-level fourth suit, responder's new suits
   after opener's 2NT and 2M rebids, the invitational jump preference after 1NT.
   Standard. The 1M-1NT-2NT continuations are already on `gf-after-2nt`.
7. **Responses to 1m: splinters (1m-3x, 1m-4x) and the preemptive 4M**: conventions both
   GIB and Brill play; Rick's call for each card.
8. **Brill-card specifics** (Rick): the impossible 2♠, Minor-transfer continuations after
   1NT-3♣, the 3m jump in their minor as a preempt, and reading Brill's 1m-2M as weak
   (our Brill card plays Soloway).

Caveats: the tree is our own, so a position Brill reaches by its own meanings (say,
1NT-3♣ as a minor transfer) may mean something else here; the cross-check asks Brill
about the call sequence, not about our meanings. Vulnerability none throughout.
WBridge5's evidence is thin (1,199 deals).
