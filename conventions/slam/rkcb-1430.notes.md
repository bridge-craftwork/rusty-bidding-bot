# rkcb-1430 (`rkcb-1430.bid`): notes

Roman Keycard Blackwood, 1430 answers, keyed off auction state (agreed
trump, game force) rather than auction patterns. Cases: `rkcb-1430.test`.

## Guidance

- Keycards are tracked with the **five-keycard limit**: partner's answer is
  combined with my own count, so "1 or 4" facing my two is 1.
- **When the answer stays ambiguous**, the asker signs off in five of the
  trump suit, and the answerer corrects to six holding the higher count.
- Never pass a keycard answer (unless it is five of our suit, a sign-off):
  that is a mistake, not a judgment difference.

## Evidence from BBA

Texas_Transfer (500 boards), after 4NT:

- `5C` then BBA's 5D is a queen ask (4 boards); we bid six directly.
- `5S` (two and the queen) then BBA bids 7S with 15–20 (5 boards); we bid 6S.
- `5D` ("0 or 3") then BBA bids six with 13–16, reading it as 3 (9 boards).

## Accepted differences from BBA

- The ambiguous "0 or 3": BBA assumes the count that fits; we sign off in
  five and let partner correct (Rick's rule).

## When to ask

The two-branch test in the `# The ask` block is shared with
`blackwood.bid` and is written up once in **`slam-catch.notes.md`**. It
is Rick's rule: **33 combined support points** with the agreed suit as
trump (`we.tp(trump)`), read against partner's maximum when partner is
limited and against partner's floor in a game force, plus at most four
losers and **no two bare side suits**.

It replaced `when slam_try` (31+ combined HCP) and, with it, the
`we.forcing = game` requirement in the ask context: the module also
serves auctions that merely agree a trump suit, which is where the drops
were.

On the 21GF cards, which have `Cue bid = 1`, a hand with **one** bare
side suit does not come here at all: it control-bids first
(`control-bids.bid`) and returns to 4NT only once partner has shown a
control above it. That is the third rule at priority 5, gated on
`asked control(t), answered control(u)` — partner control-bid *and* was
answering mine, so I am the hand that opened the dialogue and the one
that takes charge. Removing it costs 0.9 points of contract agreement
and 830 IMPs on the slam scenarios, so it is the part of the dialogue
that earns its keep.

## Shared conditions named in slam-entry.bid (2026-09-30)

The long conditions of rkcb-1430.bid (up to 1,300 characters a line) are now
named conditions in `slam/slam-entry.bid`: `slam_values`,
`slam_values_limited`, `controlled(x)`, `first_round(x)`,
`side_suit_uncontrolled`, `two_bare_suits`, `denials_promised`,
`all_covered` and their variants. Each is exactly the condition it
replaced; a definition reads `style` from slam-entry.bid, which names the
same card field and default (`slam.cue_bids.style`,
`first_or_second_round`). The full corpus bids identically
(docs/JUDGMENT-LAYER.md, Phase 0). `two_bare_suits` uses the new
`bare(x)` (a side suit of 2+ cards without the ace or king), which, like
`has`, is not recorded when a call is read, so the ask's negative
inference is unchanged; `bare_suits <= 1` would say the same but would
add that denial, a change to measure on its own.

## A fit known but not agreed (2026-09-30)

With no trump suit set, a public fit in a major (eight shown between us,
or partner's six-card suit) and slam values on partner's floor (or his
top when he has limited his hand, as a preempt does), 4NT agrees the
major and asks (`sets trump=x`). Uncontested only. Evidence and figures:
slam-entry.notes.md, "Phase 1".

## The keycard suit (Rick, 2026-10-03)

> Normally a major fit will replace a minor fit.

> A 4NT with no prior NT bids is normally keycard regardless, and if
> there hasn't been suit agreement, it's for the last bid suit. So
> 1H-1S-2H-4NT would be keycard for H.

For the cards that play Blackwood or RKCB; with neither (bare SAYC)
4NT stays natural or quantitative. 1NT-2D-2H-4NT stays quantitative:
notrump has been bid and hearts are not agreed (fit.notes.md, Rick
2026-10-02).

**The rule, as written** (slam-entry.bid, "The keycard suit"): the
keycard suit is

1. the trump suit a rule has set, unless it is a minor and a major has
   since been agreed by what the two hands have shown
   (`major_over_minor`: 1S-2C-3C-4S-4NT asks in spades, and makes them
   trump);
2. with none set, a suit agreed by the shown minimums (eight between
   us; a major before a minor), or partner's own six-card major (the
   existing `shown_fit` asks);
3. with no agreement and no notrump bid by us (`our_notrump`), the last
   suit our side bid: partner's last call, when it was a natural bid in
   a suit (two or more cards shown in it; 2C-2D-4NT, where 2D is a
   waiting bid, stays the natural 28+). Rick's example has opener's 2H
   as the last suit; "our side" because in competition the last suit
   bid at the table may be theirs. In practice it is partner's last
   suit: my own last suit is never the last one before my 4NT unless
   partner passed or doubled, which this does not cover.

`our_notrump` approximates "our side has bid notrump", since the
language keeps no call history: one of our last calls was notrump, or
either hand is known balanced (what a notrump opening, rebid or overcall
shows). It is written with `!(maybe !partner.balanced)`, "known", so
that the reading (docs/LANGUAGE.md §8: a public part must be known true)
can rule the keycard rules out over 1NT-2D-2H. `me.last` counts as the
caller's own hand there and would leave the rule possible: bare `last`
is public.

**Where the ask is bid.** The rules for 2 and 3 that are new (any suit
as the last suit, a minor agreed by the shown minimums) rank below the
descriptive calls (priority −1, as the game-force ask): at priority 1,
with a private fit, they asked where the sequence modules agree slowly
(slam-entry.test: 1C-2S-3S, 1H-2S-3S). A minor ask also needs 30 HCP on
partner's floor: with the count alone a 17-count asked facing
1D-1NT-2S-3D's preference (rebids.test). The major-over-minor ask ranks
as the agreeing asks (priority 1). Uncontested only, as the agreeing
asks: in competition a 4NT with no trump set still has no rule.

**Tried and dropped: the same suits in competition.** Rick's "keycard
regardless" covers competition too. Written as a fallback (priority
−20, meant to give the reading only), the ask still replaced passes
over partner's 4M in competition (P 4H P: P → 4NT, 24 corpus boards
−95): corpus contract errors +36 but errors −32 (−63 / +31), 21GF +37
and −37 (−54 / +17), doubling errors −68 and −74; distance from par
+43 and +62. The halves disagree and the errors fall; not kept. The
reading in competition is an open question below.

## Our side's notrump, from the whole auction (2026-10-03)

Rick asked for the engine change: "make the engine change to keep longer
history". `our_notrump` could only see our last two calls, so
1C-1S-1NT-2D-2H-4NT read the 4NT as keycard for hearts although our side
had bid notrump. The engine now records, beside each call, whether its
rule marked it artificial, and a new public term `named(x)` (for a seat,
or `we.named(x)` for our side) says whether a natural bid in strain x was
made at any point; artificial calls (a transfer, a 2NT feature ask) do
not count. `our_notrump` is `we.named(N)` or a shown balanced hand.

That left such 4NTs with no reading at all: the quantitative 4NT is
written auction by auction in the notrump modules. slam-entry.bid now has
a fallback, `we.named(N)`, no suit agreed, nothing asked, uncontested:
4NT quantitative, 31-32 between us, answered by one-nt.bid's
`when asked quant`. It is ranked below every pass (priority -60), so it
is for reading: at a higher rank it took over the modules' own 3NT and
pass choices (one-nt.test, over-3nt.test, responder-rebids.test).

**Watch: the unusual 4NT** (Rick, 2026-10-03): "there's another use for
4NT, unusual, and it comes up in different scenarios, mainly by
intervenors who have not found a fit. We don't need to implement this
now, but should watch out for it." The keycard default and this fallback
apply only when the opponents have not bid (`!they.bid`), so an
intervenor's 4NT is not read as either. When 4NT is extended to
competition (open question 1 above), the unusual 4NT must come first.

## The 5NT king ask (2026-10-05)

With `slam.king_ask.five_nt` (every PBS card), 5NT after the answer
promises all five keycards and the trump queen (mine, or partner's 5♠
answer) and asks for side kings by number: 6♣ none, 6♦ one, 6♥ two,
6♠ three. The asker bids seven with every side king between us, or two
and 33 HCP on partner's floor; else six (6NT when the answer passed
six of a minor). BBA answers the same way (601 "K=1" at 6♦, 434 "K=0"
at 6♣ over the corpus). The 35-HCP grand still comes first.

Fixed on the way: `partner.has(Q,t)` is never known (the engine does
not track another hand's honours), so the grand rule never saw
partner's 5♠ "two and the queen"; it now reads `partner.last = 5S`.

Open: the specific-king style and `slam.king_ask.next_step` are not
built; the answerer never jumps to seven on his own.
